# Sprint Retrospectives

## Purpose

The Sprint Retrospective is a structured meeting held after the Sprint Review, before the next Sprint Planning. Its purpose is to inspect and adapt the team's *process*, not the product.

The goal is continuous improvement: identify what is working, what is not, and what the team will commit to doing differently next sprint.

## Who Attends

- All Scrum Team members (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team)
- External stakeholders do not typically attend

## Timeboxes

- 1-week sprint: 45-minute retro
- 2-week sprint: 90-minute retro
- 3–4-week sprint: up to 3-hour retro

## Core Questions

The most common retro format uses three questions:

1. **What went well?** — What should we continue doing?
2. **What could be improved?** — What caused friction, delay, or frustration?
3. **What will we commit to improving next sprint?** — Specific, actionable items

## Popular Retro Formats

### Start / Stop / Continue
- **Start**: Things we should begin doing
- **Stop**: Things we should stop doing (they're causing harm or waste)
- **Continue**: Things that are working and should be kept

### 4Ls
- **Liked**: What we enjoyed or found valuable
- **Learned**: New insights or knowledge gained
- **Lacked**: What was missing
- **Longed For**: What the team wishes it had

### Sailboat / Speed Boat
- **Wind** (anchors): What is slowing us down?
- **Sails**: What is pushing us forward?
- **Rocks** (risks): What might sink us?
- **Sunny island** (goal): What are we sailing toward?

### Mad / Sad / Glad
- **Mad**: What frustrated the team?
- **Sad**: What disappointed the team?
- **Glad**: What made the team happy or proud?

## Retro Anti-Patterns to Avoid

| Anti-pattern | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Blame culture | People defend instead of improve | Establish "blameless post-mortems" norm |
| Same action items repeated | Nothing changes | Time-box action items; assign owners |
| Only complaints, no positives | Team demoralization | Always start with "what went well" |
| No follow-up | Actions never tracked | Add retro actions to next sprint backlog |
| PM dominates | Team voice suppressed | Scrum Master facilitates; PM observes |
| Too long | Engagement drops | Timebox ruthlessly |

## Action Items from Retrospectives

Retro action items should be:
- **Specific** — "Improve PR review" is not specific. "Review all PRs within 4 hours of submission" is.
- **Owned** — Assign a person responsible for each action
- **Trackable** — Add to next sprint backlog or team agreement
- **Reviewed** — Check at the start of the next retro

## Patterns to Watch for in Retro Data

If the same issues appear in multiple consecutive retrospectives:
- **Recurring blockers** → Systemic issue, needs escalation beyond the team
- **Process complaints** → Team may need Agile coaching or tooling upgrade
- **Morale issues** → Look at workload distribution, meeting load, or unclear goals
- **Dependency complaints** → PM should address cross-team coordination

## Retro Metrics

Track retro output over time to see if the process is improving:

| Metric | What to Track |
|--------|--------------|
| Action item completion rate | % of last sprint's retro actions completed |
| Recurring issues | How many retro themes repeat across sprints |
| Sentiment trend | Team satisfaction score over time |
| Velocity correlation | Does velocity improve after process changes? |

## The PM's Role in Retrospectives

A PMO or PM should:
- Review retro outputs for systemic patterns across teams
- Escalate team blockers that cannot be resolved at the team level
- Report on retro health as part of sprint reporting to stakeholders
- Not run the retro — that is the Scrum Master's responsibility
- Ensure retro action items are visible and tracked
